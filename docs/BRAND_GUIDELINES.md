# RudraNāda Brand & Editorial Bible

**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Pratap Sharma  
**Status:** Source of truth  
**Last updated:** 22 September 2026

> **Ancient in spirit. Contemporary in execution.**

This document defines how RudraNāda should look, sound and behave across the website, music, editorial, social media, artwork, thumbnails and future products.

## 1. Brand in one sentence

**RudraNāda brings India’s epics, devotional traditions and sacred sound into the present through original music, thoughtful storytelling and spiritually grounded interpretation.**

RudraNāda is not only a music channel, mythology site or religion blog. Music is often the doorway. The larger purpose is to help people listen, remember, understand and go deeper.

## 2. Core experience

### Music → Story → Meaning

A strong RudraNāda journey often moves through three layers:

1. **Music** creates emotional entry.
2. **Story** gives context.
3. **Meaning** invites reflection, inquiry or practice.

Examples:
- Uth Parth → Arjuna’s crisis → Bhagavad Gita → dharma and action.
- Mahakali → Devi traditions → fear, protection, dissolution and surrender.
- Ram Naam → nāma-japa → Rama/Hanuman bhakti → remembrance and service.
- Guru Bodha → teacher and student → inquiry → lived understanding.

## 3. Brand promise

RudraNāda should make ancient material feel alive without making it shallow.

We will:
- create devotional art with reverence;
- make difficult stories easier to enter without flattening them;
- distinguish scripture, later tradition, regional retelling and RudraNāda interpretation;
- write for intelligent readers without sounding academic for its own sake;
- use modern design without turning sacred material into lifestyle decoration;
- use SEO and AI-search structure to improve clarity, never to create empty content.

## 4. Personality

RudraNāda should feel:
- devotional;
- cinematic;
- reflective;
- source-aware;
- emotionally powerful when the subject calls for it;
- calm when stillness is the point;
- spiritually Indian without relying on visual clichés;
- respectful of regional and textual variation;
- curious rather than preachy;
- accessible without becoming simplistic.

## 5. What RudraNāda is not

Do not make RudraNāda feel like:
- a generic “Hindu facts” site;
- a mythology trivia mill;
- a quote-card spirituality account;
- calendar-art imitation;
- generic Bollywood devotion;
- random mass shouting;
- saffron-and-gold luxury branding;
- invented Sanskrit;
- scripture mixed invisibly with artistic lyrics;
- SEO filler;
- AI-generated sludge;
- guru personality worship.

If something looks devotional only because it contains a temple, flame, lotus, trident and gold glow, it needs another idea.

# VISUAL IDENTITY

## 6. Logo system

### Primary mark
Use `/public/rudranaada-logo.svg`.

Use for:
- website header;
- footer;
- partner or press assets;
- presentation covers;
- branded editorial assets.

### Compact mark
Use `/app/icon.svg`.

Use for:
- favicon;
- app icon;
- small avatars;
- compact brand moments.

### Clear space
Keep at least roughly **25% of the mark’s height** clear around the primary lockup.

### Minimum digital size
- Full lockup: 100 px wide minimum.
- Header preferred range: 108–132 px.
- Footer preferred range: 160–200 px.
- Compact mark: 24 px minimum unless specifically redrawn for smaller use.

### Never
- stretch or squash;
- recolour individual pieces arbitrarily;
- add glow, bevel or shadow;
- redraw the mark;
- place it on a noisy image without protection;
- add another “RudraNāda” wordmark beside it;
- use a screenshot of the logo when the SVG exists.

## 7. Colour system

RudraNāda is primarily a **light editorial brand**.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Warm Ivory | `#FBFAF7` | General website background |
| Pure White | `#FFFFFF` | Blogs, stories, long-form reading surfaces, cards |
| Ink | `#211D18` | Primary text |
| Muted Earth | `#74695E` | Supporting text |
| Sacred Gold | `#A66B16` | Accent |
| Deep Gold | `#81500F` | Links and restrained emphasis |
| Sand | `#F3EFE8` | Footer / secondary surface |

### Non-negotiable reading rule

**All long-form editorial pages use a pure white `#FFFFFF` reading canvas.**

Do not use:
- radiant gold;
- gold gradients;
- beige washes behind article text;
- glowing devotional backgrounds;
- decorative texture behind body copy.

Gold is an accent, not a reading surface.

Use gold only for:
- eyebrow labels;
- links;
- restrained dividers;
- selected states;
- primary buttons;
- small decorative details.

## 8. Typography

### Display/editorial
Current: **Georgia, serif**

Use for:
- H1;
- H2;
- editorial section titles;
- important card titles.

Character:
- literary;
- calm;
- serious;
- timeless;
- not ornamental.

### Interface/support
Current: **Inter / system sans-serif**

Use for:
- navigation;
- metadata;
- labels;
- buttons;
- captions;
- filters;
- UI controls.

### Long-form body
A serif reading voice is appropriate for essays and stories. Paragraphs must remain generous and breathable.

### Type hierarchy
- Homepage hero: largest type, short copy only.
- Hub/page hero: smaller than homepage hero, usually 1–3 lines.
- Research article H1: roughly 2.5–3.65 rem on desktop; aim for 2–4 lines.
- Article H2: roughly 1.8–2.35 rem.
- Body: roughly 16–18 px equivalent with 1.65–1.8 line height.

### Never
- let a long article title fill most of the first viewport;
- reuse homepage hero typography for blogs;
- set body paragraphs across the full screen;
- use decorative typefaces for long reading.

## 9. Reading width and layout

- Global shell: 1120 px preferred, 1180 px ceiling.
- Article title/media wrapper: up to ~920 px.
- Main reading column: ~760 px.
- Ideal paragraph line length: 60–75 characters.
- Desktop gutters: at least 24 px.
- Mobile gutters: at least 14 px.

Section headings and supporting copy must share a deliberate grid. Do not bottom-align explanatory text against multi-line headings.

## 10. Spacing

Use a restrained 4/8-based rhythm.

Preferred spacing families:
- 4
- 8
- 12
- 16
- 24
- 32
- 48
- 64
- 80
- 96

Do not invent a new spacing value for every component.

## 11. Cards

Cards should:
- have white surfaces;
- use light borders;
- use subtle shadow only where it improves separation;
- keep padding consistent;
- avoid giant empty fixed heights;
- align titles and metadata predictably.

Avoid:
- glassmorphism;
- glowing borders;
- gold gradient cards;
- excessive rounding;
- floating card stacks that compete with the content.

## 12. Images

RudraNāda imagery should feel:
- cinematic;
- bold;
- composed;
- sacred without becoming kitsch;
- emotionally specific to the story.

Avoid:
- oversaturated orange/gold everything;
- excessive divine glow;
- random particle effects;
- calendar-art clutter;
- deity faces that feel plastic or generic;
- stock-photo spirituality;
- watermarked imagery.

For editorial articles:
- use a strong featured image;
- place images at natural narrative breaks;
- write useful captions where context helps;
- avoid decorative images that add no meaning.

# EDITORIAL VOICE

## 13. Voice

RudraNāda writing should sound like a strong contemporary writer who understands the spiritual tradition.

It should be:
- clear;
- warm;
- grounded;
- intelligent;
- spiritually literate;
- specific;
- human.

It should not sound:
- corporate;
- SEO-generated;
- pseudo-mystical;
- preachy;
- over-polished;
- like an AI summarizing a Wikipedia page.

### Good
“Arjuna does not begin the Gita as a calm philosopher. His hand trembles.”

### Weak
“The Bhagavad Gita offers timeless wisdom for modern life and continues to inspire millions worldwide.”

Prefer concrete moments over generic reverence.

## 14. Headline rule

A heading should make a person want to read the next paragraph.

Avoid UX labels such as:
- Overview;
- Five entry points;
- How we read it;
- What this page contains.

Prefer:
- “First, Arjuna’s hand begins to tremble.”
- “Five ways into the same war.”
- “A teaching is not complete because it was heard.”

## 15. Editorial integrity

Always distinguish:
1. **Primary text / scripture**
2. **Traditional commentary**
3. **Later retelling**
4. **Regional tradition**
5. **Popular belief**
6. **Scholarly interpretation**
7. **RudraNāda artistic interpretation**

Never:
- invent a verse;
- invent Sanskrit;
- turn a lyric paraphrase into scripture;
- imply a regional custom is universal;
- erase disagreements between traditions.

## 16. Sanskrit and transliteration

- Use familiar English spellings where natural.
- Use diacritics when precision materially helps.
- Define Sanskrit terms in plain English on first use.
- Do not overload general-reader articles with academic transliteration.
- Preserve sacred names carefully.

## 17. Long-form article standard

All long-form editorial content should follow this hierarchy when appropriate:

1. Direct answer opening
2. **In brief**
3. 3–5 key takeaways
4. Detailed story / explanation
5. Clear, descriptive H2s
6. Visual breaks
7. Internal links
8. Visible FAQ
9. Research references
10. Author attribution

### AI-search principle
Write sections so a useful paragraph can stand on its own when extracted, without making every paragraph sound like a search snippet.

### FAQ
Use 5–8 genuinely useful questions for substantial research articles when the topic supports them.

FAQ content is for human clarity and machine extraction. Do not assume FAQ markup creates ranking benefits.

## 18. Sources

Research references should be visible as **plain-text citations/references** on RudraNāda unless there is a strong editorial reason to send the reader away.

Prefer:
- primary texts;
- established translations;
- critical editions;
- traditional commentaries;
- reputable academic sources;
- credible cultural/government institutions.

Sources exist to establish trust, not decorate the article.

# PRODUCT & UI

## 20. Navigation

Desktop:
- logo left;
- primary navigation next;
- YouTube CTA distinct from site navigation.

Mobile:
- use an actual menu;
- never simply hide navigation.

Footer:
- separate **Explore** from **Follow**.
- Follow always contains:
  - YouTube
  - Instagram
  - X

RSS belongs under Explore/utility, not social.

## 21. Buttons

Primary:
- Sacred Gold background;
- white text;
- strong contrast.

Secondary:
- white/transparent background;
- Ink text;
- subtle border.

Avoid multiple competing primary buttons in one section.

## 22. Responsive behaviour

Every design should be checked at:
- ~1440 px desktop;
- ~1024 px laptop/tablet landscape;
- ~768 px tablet;
- ~390 px phone.

Do not rely only on browser shrinking. Check:
- headline wrapping;
- nav;
- card stacking;
- CTA wrapping;
- image cropping;
- article line length;
- footer grouping;
- tap targets.

# MUSIC IDENTITY

## 23. RudraNāda sound

The music should feel:
- world-class cinematic;
- devotional first;
- emotionally immediate;
- rooted without being predictable;
- willing to use instruments outside India when they serve the piece;
- rhythmically memorable;
- vocally distinctive.

Avoid:
- generic Bollywood romantic progression;
- generic “mass” drums;
- random shouting;
- crowd screams used as energy shortcuts;
- cheap trailer-music clichés;
- overproduced devotional-pop vocals;
- pronunciation that sounds foreign to the language.

## 24. Energy

High energy does not mean noise.

Intensity can come from:
- rhythm;
- silence;
- repetition;
- orchestration;
- low-frequency movement;
- vocal texture;
- unexpected instrumentation;
- a strong lyrical hook.

A RudraNāda “war cry” should feel earned, not pasted on.

- tiny titles;

# GOVERNANCE

## 27. Decision test

Before publishing or designing, ask:

1. Does this feel like RudraNāda or generic devotional content?
2. Is the sacred material treated with care?
3. Can a reader understand what is scripture and what is interpretation?
4. Is the design easy to read before it is beautiful?
5. Is gold acting as an accent or taking over the page?
6. Does the piece have a clear reason to exist?
7. Would this still feel good without SEO traffic?
8. Does it feel ancient in spirit and contemporary in execution?

If several answers are “no,” revise before publishing.
